**Investment Thesis**: The direction is clear, and the debate never challenged it. All three analysts accept the business evidence. Revenue grew about 85% year over year to $81.6B. Gross margin rose to 74.9% and operating margin to 65.6%. Operating income was $53.5B, about 2.5x a year earlier, so growth holds up even after stripping out the roughly $4.8B of non-operating gains. Quarterly free cash flow was about $48.6B, and leverage is about 0.33x. The trend also points up: price of $217.32 is above a rising 200-day SMA at $194.79 and a 50-day SMA at $206.89 that is turning up, and the MACD histogram is still positive. Even the bear and the Conservative argued about entry and size, not about whether to own the stock. That is why the rating has a positive tilt rather than Hold.

On sizing and execution, the Conservative and Neutral analysts made the better arguments. First, momentum is still weakening. The histogram fell from 2.49 to 0.59 over six sessions, RSI has made lower highs, price is below the 10-day EMA, and there have been three lower closes in a row. Second, a $204 stop is only about 1.2 ATR (ATR is $6.58) below a $212 entry, so normal daily moves can trigger it. Third, the Aggressive analyst's 3:1 reward-to-risk depends on a full retest of $235. The realistic near-term target is $219–225, which pays about 7–13 points for 8 points of risk. Fourth, important information is missing: there is no current multiple or market cap, no macro, rates or news data, no Q4 FY26 figures, and the next earnings date is unknown. A stop does not protect against an overnight gap. Fifth, earnings quality is open to question: net income exceeded operating income by about $4.8B, and operating cash flow came in about $8B below net income.

The Aggressive case for a bigger first tranche, and for pressing the position as it works, is rejected. Adding above $219 while keeping the stop at $204 raises the dollar at risk at the very point where momentum is weakest. The Conservative's trigger of a close above $212.14 is also rejected, because that is no real confirmation. The plan follows the Neutral version: a smaller starter, an exit on a closing break of the 50-day SMA rather than a tight fixed stop, size set so that a 2-ATR move (about 13 points) or an earnings gap stays within normal risk limits, and the stop moved up after the second tranche goes on.

What would change the call:
- A daily close below $212 pauses new buying.
- A close below about $205 means going back to a standard 1.0x weight, or exiting the starter.
- A break of the 200-day SMA ($194.79) or the $189.80 low means the thesis has failed. Cut to Underweight (about 0.5x).
- If the valuation check shows the price already assumes the best case, or the 10-Q shows earnings rely on gains from stakes in customers, cap the position at 1.0x or lower.
- On the upside, a close above $225 with momentum turning up again, plus a reasonable forward multiple, would justify moving to the full 1.25x and treating the call as a Buy.
