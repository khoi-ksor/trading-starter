**Investment Thesis**: After weighing the debate, the evidence is still balanced. Neither side makes a directional case strong enough to act on.

The case for owning the business is real but dated. Revenue was $81.6B, up about 85% year over year. Gross margin was about 75%, operating margin about 66%, and operating cash flow about $50B. No analyst disputes these figures, and they are why I reject Underweight or Sell. The Conservative analyst's strongest point was not rebutted, though: the latest quarter ends April 26, and the slide from 235.20 to 189.80 started in mid-May. The fundamentals cannot show whether the market has already priced in a slowdown. The Aggressive analyst's claim that 'the numbers haven't deteriorated' therefore rests on no data from after April. Their roughly $48B free cash flow figure also depends on a capex number whose column alignment the research plan flagged as uncertain.

The 'discount' claim fails. No analyst has a P/E, EV/Sales or forward estimate, and the Neutral and Conservative analysts both correctly say that a lower price is not the same as a cheaper stock. The Aggressive analyst's own reading undercuts their entry: they call the 147.7M-share selloff distribution, not capitulation, which means sellers aren't done. Buying a third of a position now and adding into the 189–192 zone that is failing is averaging into a live breakdown.

The technical damage is confirmed. The close at 189.80 is below the 200-day (192.59), the 10-day EMA (200.89), the 50-day (206.82) and the Bollinger middle band (202.90), with MACD negative. The bull's own invalidation level, a close below the 200-day, has already been hit.

Reward and risk are roughly even, and the stop debate shapes the plan. The 10-day EMA is about 5.8% above the close. The Neutral analyst fairly noted that using 182 instead of the 2-ATR level at 174.76 improves the ratio. Even so, the upside targets are resistance levels and the cause of the drop is unknown, so neither ratio is compelling. I accept the point that the Aggressive and Neutral analysts both made: a stop at 188.9, about 0.9 points or roughly 0.1 ATR below the close, is noise rather than risk control. So 188.9 is not a standalone sell trigger. The downgrade requires a close below it followed by a close below about 182, roughly one ATR lower. One caution: the Neutral analyst's '189.65' level has no stated basis in the data, so I don't use it.

Why Hold rather than Overweight: the Neutral analyst's risk-budgeted staging is the most sensible framework. Starting it before the 200-day is reclaimed, though, means buying beneath a broken level with no valuation, no news and no macro inputs. The 200-day reclaim is only about 1.5% away, so waiting for it costs little and confirms that buyers are back.

What would change the call:
- **Upgrade to Overweight.** Price closes back above 192.59, then reclaims about 201 and about 207 on volume, with MACD turning positive and RSI above 50. Primary sources must also show the July drop was not company-specific, and valuation has to look reasonable against growth.
- **Downgrade to Underweight.** A close below 188.9 followed by a close below about 182.
- **Review for Sell.** A close below about 175, or confirmed hyperscaler order cuts or new export restrictions.

Missing evidence that must be filled before any meaningful increase: valuation multiples and forward estimates, news explaining the July drop, macro inputs (no FRED key is set), the breakdown of the doubled asset base ($259.5B), the source of the roughly $4.8B non-operating gain, and confirmed capex and free cash flow figures.
