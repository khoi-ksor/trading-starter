**Investment Thesis**: All three risk analysts agree on Hold. They differ only on whether to add early (aggressive) or trim early (conservative). After weighing both, neither change is justified.

Why not add: The aggressive case for adding on a 223 close rests on one precedent, the September 14 low at 210.96 followed by a rally to 239.24. Both the conservative and neutral analysts showed why that is weak support. It is a single data point, and the technical report says the September RSI low "did not mark a floor for long." Today price sits below the 10 EMA, the 50 SMA (223.09) and the VWMA all at once. MACD is below its signal line with the histogram at -1.27 and widening, and the 10-14 drop came on heavier volume (118.7M shares) than the bounce before it. A close back above 223 would still leave price under 228.67 and 231.23, so adding there is a bet on a retest, not a confirmed trend. The falling ATR (5.82, down from 7.02) says a bigger move is likely but not which way, so it can't justify a directional add. The 1.7:1 reward-to-risk to 239.24 also overstates the upside, because the 10 EMA, middle Bollinger band (228.69) and the 230 cluster all sit overhead. The capitulation reading from Reddit comes from a four-post sample and is anecdote. Valuation multiples were withheld, so nobody can show the stock is cheap.

Why not cut: The bear-leaning evidence doesn't win decisively either. Revenue was about $96.2B, up about 106% year over year. Gross margin is 75%, operating margin is 66%, six-month operating cash flow is about $74B against about $4.4B of capex, and the current ratio is about 4.6x. Price is still about 9% above the 200 SMA. The conservative case for trimming now depends on macro risk nobody has measured. FRED failed, the 10-year's distance from the 5.9% BofA threshold is unknown, and the bullish and bearish headlines are both unverified. As the neutral analyst pointed out, the first trim trigger (217.8) is only about 1.6% below the 221.30 close. A pre-emptive trim buys little protection, and the plan already has close-based trim levels. The conservative claim that September looked structurally different also relies on September indicator values we don't have.

What's missing: valuation data (P/E, EV/Sales, market cap), confirmation of how the latest quarters' figures were mapped, and the macro readings (10-year yield, CPI). With strong fundamentals, a short-term downtrend and no valuation anchor, the evidence is balanced, so this is a rules-based Hold rather than a directional call.

Risk management: Gap risk through a close-based stop is real. A fall to the 200 SMA would cost about 9.6% on the position. If your book can't absorb that, cut weight now. That is a portfolio-level choice, not a signal. If you hold a lot of high-multiple tech, an optional roughly 210/195 put spread through earnings is a reasonable hedge.
