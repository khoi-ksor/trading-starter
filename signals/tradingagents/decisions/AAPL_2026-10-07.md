**Rating**: Overweight

**Executive Summary**: Add to AAPL in stages rather than buying a full position now. Tranche 1 is about one-third of a standard allocation, bought only on a daily close above $334 or on a limit fill at $322–326. Tranche 2 is another third. It needs a close above $341.90, MACD back above its signal line, and a confirmed cause for the July 31 drop. Fill it at half size if that cause is still unknown. Tranche 3 waits for the fiscal Q4 report. Keep total exposure at two-thirds of a standard allocation or less before that report. Cut the starter on a daily close below $322, exit fully below $309, and plan to hold for 3–6 months.

**Investment Thesis**: The business case is clearly positive, and none of the three analysts disputed it. Revenue is up about 16% year over year, both in the latest quarter and over nine months. Gross margin rose from 46.5% to 50.1%, and operating margin from 30.0% to 32.6%. Nine-month operating cash flow grew 43% to $117B, more than net income, with about $110B of free cash flow. The long-term trend also holds: the price ($336.67) is above the 50-day average ($322.24) and a rising 200-day average ($289.73). That is enough to lean positive, so Hold would be too passive.

The Aggressive case wins on whether to own the stock, but not on how much to buy. Its key claim, that the market has 'already absorbed' the July 31 drop of 7.4% on about 4x normal volume, was the weakest point in the debate. The Conservative and Neutral analysts were right that a recovery tells us nothing about the cause. July 31 may have been the reaction to fiscal Q3 results or guidance, which would mean the market reacted to the very growth numbers the bull cites. Valuation data is missing entirely, so 'a rich multiple can be justified' is an assumption, not a finding. The Aggressive analyst's 3.6% risk figure also only applies to one tranche stopped at $322. A full position bought near $334 and stopped at $309 loses about 7.5%, and the July gap shows stops can be jumped. I reject the proposal to drop the MACD condition and add more before earnings.

The Conservative analyst overreaches the other way. The MACD histogram has narrowed from -1.07 to -0.82, RSI at 57.7 is not overbought, and price has stayed in a $329–341 range since the September 25 high. That looks like a pause, not a breakdown, so refusing to buy at all is not justified.

The Neutral analyst's refined plan is the best version of the trade, and I'm adopting it. It also fixes the order of the stop rules:
- If Tranche 1 is stopped below $322 (including a gap below that level, which means exiting at the next available price), do not buy Tranche 2 until the price closes back above $334 and the breakout conditions are met.
- $309 is where the thesis is wrong: exit everything and don't buy back before the Q4 report.
- With exposure capped at two-thirds, a fall to $309 costs about 5% of a standard allocation, which is affordable.

Before adding anything beyond Tranche 1, check three things: the cause of the July 31 drop, current valuation (forward P/E and EV/FCF compared with Apple's own history), and the FOMC and CPI calendar.

What would change the call:
- Downgrade to Hold or Underweight if the July 31 cause turns out to be structural (regulatory or legal trouble, or the loss of a product line), if valuation is far above Apple's historical range, if the Q4 report shows growth slowing well below the mid-teens or margins shrinking, or on a close below $309.
- Upgrade to Buy if the Q4 report confirms growth near the mid-teens with flat or rising margins, valuation checks out as reasonable, and the price breaks above $341.90 with MACD confirming.

Still missing: valuation, news, sentiment, macro data, and fiscal Q4 FY25 results. No price target is set because there is no valuation data to base one on.
