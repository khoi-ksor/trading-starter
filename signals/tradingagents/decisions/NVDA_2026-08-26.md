**Executive Summary**: Build NVDA exposure gradually. Start with one-third to one-half of a standard position, split between about $209 and either a pullback to $203-207 or a close back above about $214-215.50 on solid volume. Use a closing stop near $200 (about 1.5 ATR). Cut back to a starter position on a close below $200 and exit fully on a close below the 200 SMA ($195.15). Do not go past a full standard position until valuation, the missing January 2026 quarter and the liability increase have been checked. Horizon is 2-4 months, with the prior closing high of about $225 as the first target.

**Investment Thesis**: The business case is clearly the stronger one, and none of the three risk analysts disputed it. Revenue is about $96B, up about 106% from a year ago and about 18% from last quarter. Year-over-year growth sped up from about 85% in the April quarter. Gross margin is 75% and operating margin is 66%. Six-month operating cash flow is $74.4B and the current ratio is about 4.6. Operating income rose roughly in line with revenue, so the April quarter, when net income ran ahead of operating income because of non-operating gains, does not point to weak core earnings. The longer trend also holds: price (about $209.43) is above the rising 50 SMA ($207.52) and the 200 SMA ($195.15), and ATR has dropped from 7.87 to 6.25.

The conservative analyst won on timing, which is why this is Overweight and not a full Buy. Price is below the 10 EMA ($214.42) and the Bollinger middle line ($215.32). The MACD histogram has fallen from +2.49 to -1.53 and is still widening. There was a heavy-volume down day on 179.9M shares, against a usual 90-140M. Valuation, macro and sentiment data are all missing, so nobody can claim the strong numbers are already priced in. The conservative analyst also correctly caught two errors by the aggressive analyst: the July-August rally was about 18% (from a $189.80 close to a $225.05 close), not a near-doubling, and the $200 stop sits above the $198.79 lower band, not below it.

The conservative remedy does not hold up, though. A stop on a close below $207.5 is only about 0.3 ATR from entry, which is normal daily noise. That makes a stop-out from noise more likely, so it does not actually lower risk. The neutral analyst framed it best: smaller size justifies a smaller position with a stop placed where volatility says it belongs. It does not justify a stop inside the noise. A $200 closing stop is about 1.5 ATR and roughly 4.3-4.5% below entry. Against the roughly 7.5% move back to $225, that is a reasonable risk-reward for a starter position. Gap risk is real, so size the position so that a stop-out costs a fixed, small share of the book. For example, with a 0.5% loss budget and a stop about 4.3% away, a full position would be about 12% of the book and a one-third starter about 4%. Scale this to your own numbers.

How to act on this: if you have no position, build the starter as described above. Add up to a full position on (a) a close above about $215.50 on solid volume with the MACD histogram turning positive, or (b) a dip to $200-203 that holds and reverses. If you already hold up to a full position, keep it under the same stop and exit rules. If you hold more than a full position, trim back to full until the missing data is in. Going up to about 1.25x a full position requires reasonable forward multiples relative to growth, confirmation that the 43% rise in total liabilities (65% in current liabilities) came from operations or customer prepayments rather than borrowing, and clean free cash flow and capex figures, including the January 2026 quarter.

What would change the call: a close below $200 or a second heavy-volume down day means cutting to a starter. A close below the 200 SMA, or guidance or hyperscaler capex signals pointing to falling quarter-on-quarter revenue, means a full exit and a move toward Underweight. Confirmed reasonable valuation plus a reclaim of the 10 EMA on volume would justify upgrading to Buy.
