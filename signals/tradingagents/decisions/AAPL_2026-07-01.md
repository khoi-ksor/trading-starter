**Rating**: Overweight

**Executive Summary**: Add AAPL in stages, starting small. Open a starter of about one-fifth of a standard allocation, using a limit order between 290 and 294. Don't chase above 300, and exit on a daily close below 273. Size it so that a 10-12% gap through the stop still fits your per-trade risk budget. Add more only after the MACD histogram turns positive and the 10 EMA closes above the 50 SMA, and after valuation and the 10-Q figures have been checked. If you already hold about one-fifth of a standard allocation or more, keep the position with the 273 stop and don't add until those conditions are met. The horizon is 3-6 months, with the 314.93 prior high as the first target.

**Investment Thesis**: Nobody in the debate argues that the business or the long-term trend is breaking down. Revenue grew about 16.6% year over year and diluted EPS about 21.8%, and gross margin rose to 49.3%. Price is 294.13, above a 50 SMA (292.35) that is rising and well above a rising 200 SMA (269.73). RSI has recovered from 32 to 51, and the MACD histogram has narrowed from -2.79 to -1.26. Even the Conservative analyst says the trend 'is not broken.' The bearish case is about timing and missing data, not about the stock getting worse. That supports a long bias with careful sizing, so Hold is not the right call.

The Aggressive case overreaches, and the Conservative and Neutral analysts showed where. First, the June 26 session traded 261M shares but closed below the VWMA, and nobody knows why the volume spiked, so calling it accumulation is an assumption. Second, a starter at 294 is a bounce trade, not the breakout the Aggressive analyst described. Third, he dismissed the falling VWMA as a lagging indicator while the plan uses VWMA as a trigger for adding. Fourth, he admitted the reward to 314.93 is about 21 points against about 21 points of risk, which is roughly 1:1. On top of that, valuation, macro, sentiment and the earnings date are all missing, and the jump in stockholders' equity from about $67B to $106.5B hasn't been verified. These points justify a smaller position than the plan's one-third.

The Conservative case also goes too far. Its 1.5-ATR stop (about 282, where ATR is 8.19) sits inside normal daily swings and would likely be hit by noise. The 273 level sits just under the June 25 low of 274.91, which is where the trade is clearly wrong. Being unable to run the valuation check is a reason to size down, not to stay out entirely. Waiting for full confirmation probably means paying 305-315, which hurts the reward-to-risk about as much as entering early does.

The Neutral analyst's version settles the debate best: a starter of about one-fifth, a limit entry near the 10 EMA and 50 SMA zone (290-294), and the 273 structural stop. Size it for a gap, not just a clean fill at the stop, since the stop is about 2.6 ATR from entry and the earnings date is unknown. Adding requires both the MACD histogram turning positive and the 10 EMA closing above the 50 SMA. Price closing above the 294.63 Bollinger middle band on rising volume, or the VWMA turning up, counts as support but isn't required. After the first add, raise the stop to about 280. The last tranche needs a close above 314.93, and you should check valuation (P/E, EV/EBITDA, market cap against mid-teens growth) and the 10-Q equity and cash-flow figures before it. If valuation looks stretched, cap the position at two-thirds of standard. If earnings come out before these conditions are met, don't add ahead of the report.

What would change the call: a daily close below 273, price falling back below both the 10 EMA and the 50 SMA while the MACD histogram widens below zero, or a next quarter that shows slowing revenue or shrinking margins. Any of these would move the rating to Hold or Underweight, or mean exiting. A confirmed close above 314.93, with valuation in line with growth, would justify building up to a full or Buy-sized position.
