**Executive Summary**: Hold NVDA. Keep existing positions at no more than a standard allocation and do not add at about $197. New capital comes in stages: a starter of about one-third of a standard position is allowed only if the $190–192 support zone holds, with a stop on a daily close below $186 and sized so a stop-out costs no more than 0.5–1% of the portfolio. The rest waits for confirmation, meaning either a close above $204 on rising volume and then a reclaim of the 50 SMA (about $209.5), or a support test that holds and closes back above the 10 EMA (about $199.7). If NVDA closes below $190 on rising volume, cut to 50% of standard or less and stop adding. Horizon is 1–3 months, and going to full size depends on checking the earnings quality, valuation and export-control data that are currently missing.

**Investment Thesis**: The evidence is still balanced after weighing it, and it splits by timeframe. The business case is strong and no analyst disputed it: revenue of $81.6B (+85% year over year, +43% quarter over quarter), operating income of $53.5B (+147%), operating margin near 66%, a 3.4x current ratio and debt-to-equity of about 0.33. The short-term evidence points the other way. Price (~$197) is below the 10 EMA, the Bollinger middle band (~$204), the VWMA and the 50 SMA (~$209.5). MACD is negative, volume has been heavier on down days, and RSI has not made a higher low on the retest. The 50 SMA already turned price back in late June. Only the rising 200 SMA (~$190.5) supports the long-term trend.

The neutral analyst made the strongest case, and the decision follows it. The aggressive analyst's 3:1 trade from the $190–192 support test to a $186 stop and a $209.5 target sounds attractive, but it never estimates how likely the stock is to reach the target before the stop. A 6-point stop against a 7.07 ATR sits inside normal daily noise, and a semiconductor name can gap past a stop on export-control news. So the downside is not as 'defined' as claimed. Calling the Bollinger/ATR squeeze bullish because the fundamentals are strong is an assumption, since the technical data does not show which way it will break. The conservative analyst is right that this is not the moment to buy aggressively. But pre-emptively trimming at $204–209 is its own unconfirmed timing bet, and the stock has already fallen about 18% from $235. Missing data is a gap in the information, not bearish evidence. That rules out moving to Underweight. It also rules out Overweight, because the claim that NVDA is cheap at 20–25x rests on a price and share count nobody verified, and net income ($58.3B) running ahead of operating income ($53.5B) points to non-operating gains that may not repeat.

From the neutral analyst I take two refinements to the trader's plan. First, a small staged starter at support, sized by a fixed risk budget rather than a tight price stop. Second, a stop judged on the daily close rather than intraday, to avoid being shaken out by ordinary volatility. I accept the trader's levels otherwise: entry on confirmation around $204.24, and a risk line at $190, which falls to $186 for a starter bought at support.

What would change the call:
- **Upgrade to Overweight:** a volume-confirmed close above $204 followed by a reclaim of $209.5, plus checks showing operating income growing alongside revenue, a verified reasonable multiple, and no serious new export-control or China headwinds.
- **Downgrade to Underweight:** a daily close below $190 on rising volume, which breaks the 200 SMA and the lower band together with no clear support below, or a next report showing a revenue miss, slowing operating income, or profits that rely mostly on non-operating gains.

Still missing: a verified share count and market cap, clearly dated cash flow, receivables and inventory trends, the earnings calendar, sentiment and macro data, and news on hyperscaler spending.
