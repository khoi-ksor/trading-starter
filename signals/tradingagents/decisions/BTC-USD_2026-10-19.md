**Investment Thesis**: All three risk analysts agree on the core point: reduce exposure, but don't exit. The bear case is stronger on short-term structure, and the intact long-term trend is the reason this is not a Sell.

What decided it toward Underweight:
1) Lower highs and a falling ceiling. Highs went from 87,364 to about 87,146 to about 84,100. The upper Bollinger band fell from about 89,498 to 86,457, and price has not closed above the middle band (about 83,333) since early October.
2) The floor is wearing down. The 80,200–80,600 zone was tested on Oct 8, 15 and 18, with an intraday print at 79,736.
3) Momentum is fading. The 10-EMA has fallen for nine sessions, and VWMA (82,846) is above price, so most volume traded higher up. The MACD line fell from about 2,486 to 38 with a histogram of -456, and RSI failed to hold above 65. The 50-day SMA is still rising, but it now adds about 50–80 a day versus about 400 a day in late September.
4) The one on-chain data point, rising exchange reserves, points bearish. The odds of BTC reaching 100K by January fell 5 points to 22%.

Why not Sell: there has been no daily close below 79,700. Price is above the rising 50-day (81,398) and 200-day (72,495). BTC funding is modest, and the leverage crowding is in alts. Prediction markets give low odds of deep downside (55K at 10%, 50K at 7%). Retail sentiment is fearful, which is a mild contrarian positive. The data is also thin: no live spot, no ETF flows, no FRED macro data, and sentiment based on about 15 Reddit posts. That argues against a larger directional bet. I read the trader's 'Sell' as this measured trim, which fits Underweight.

On execution, I side with the Neutral and Conservative analysts' criticism of the trader's 83,500 'stop'. It is only about 0.7× the 14-day ATR (2,093) above entry, and it is the same level as the upgrade trigger. Treating it as a stop invites getting whipsawed and paying costs twice. So I treat 83,300–83,500 as a condition for changing the stance, not a stop-loss. I reject the Conservative idea of a vague 'review point' with no defined action. The Aggressive analyst is right that the reduction should be decided ahead of the CPI event rather than in reaction to it. Because a soft CPI print could squeeze shorts after the $820M liquidation flush, the reduction should be staged and limit-based, not one large sale into the data release.
