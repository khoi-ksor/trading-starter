**Rating**: Overweight

**Executive Summary**: Overweight AAPL, but build the position in stages and keep it smaller than the trader proposed until fiscal Q4 earnings. Start with about 20–25% of a standard allocation, preferably with limit orders in the $326–329 pullback zone. A daily close above $335 on rising volume with the MACD histogram turning up can serve as a second entry. Add near the 50 SMA ($322–325) only after two straight daily closes above $322. Keep total exposure at or below about 40% of standard before earnings, and size it so a gap to about $300 stays within your single-name loss budget. Exit on a daily close below $318, and cut exposure in half on a daily close below $322 while the MACD histogram is still negative. Horizon is 3–6 months. Go to full size only after a volume-backed break above $345 or a Q4 report with operating margin at or above 32% and revenue growth of about 15% or more.

**Investment Thesis**: What decided it: all three analysts accept the core bull evidence, and no one shows the business getting worse or the trend breaking. Revenue has grown about 16% year over year for three straight quarters. Gross margin rose about 360 bp to 50.1%, and diluted EPS rose about 29% to $2.02. Price is about 15.5% above a rising 200 SMA ($288.23) and about 3.4% above a rising 50 SMA ($322.01). The heavy-volume drops in late June and on July 31 (−7.4% on 132.5M shares) were both recovered to new highs. Even the Conservative analyst says the operating results justify owning the business eventually. The disagreement is about size and timing, not direction. That makes this a positive call with controlled size, not a Hold.

Where the Aggressive case went too far: price is about 0.4% below the 10 EMA ($333.02 close vs. $334.45), not 'a few percent' below it. Falling ATR (about 8 down to 7.25) signals that a bigger move may be coming but doesn't say which way. The 43% operating cash-flow growth comes from misaligned columns and may include working-capital timing, so it can't be treated as clean proof of efficiency. Near-term momentum really has cooled. There have been lower highs since the $345.34 peak, the MACD has dropped below its signal line (from 6.27 to 4.73), the histogram has been negative since 9/29, RSI has fallen from about 66 to 54, and price is below the VWMA ($335.34).

What the Conservative analyst got right, and what I adopted: (1) A stop based on a daily close won't hold through an earnings gap. A July-31-style drop from the entry zone would push losses well past the advertised ~4.5%, which is really about 5% from a $335 entry. (2) The exit rule in the original plan (below the 50 SMA AND MACD below zero) leaves the real exit undefined, because the MACD line is still at +4.73. (3) Pre-earnings exposure of 60–65% of standard is too much when valuation is unknown and a binary print is coming. On the other hand, the Neutral analyst correctly points out that waiting for every data gap to close means buying after the earnings gap, not avoiding it. The Conservative's own trigger, a close above $335 with the histogram turning up, is nearly the same as the plan's. So I took the Neutral's fixes: a 20–25% starter, a preference for pullback entries, two closes above $322 before adding, a price-based rule to cut half, a hard stop on a close below $318, a pre-earnings cap of about 40%, and a deliberate decision before the print. My lean is to trim back to the starter size unless price has broken above $345.

Risk math: from a $327.5 average entry, the $318 stop is about 2.9% risk and a gap to $300 is about 8.4%. Size everything to the gap figure, not the stop.

Price target: $350 over 3–6 months, a retest of and modest break above the $345 high and the upper Bollinger band near $346. A higher target would need valuation data we don't have.

What would change the call: upgrade to Buy on a break above $345 with expanding volume, or a fiscal Q4 report with operating margin at or above 32% (it has slipped from 35.4% to about 32.6%) and revenue growth of about 15% or more. Downgrade to Hold/Underweight on a daily close below $318, on operating margin shrinking in Q4, or if a verified P/E or forward multiple turns out to be extreme. In that last case, hold only the starter tranche.

Data still missing: the P/E or forward multiple, debt and marketable securities, verified cash-flow figures, the confirmed Q4 date and guidance, and news on macro, tariffs, rates and sentiment. These gaps are why the position is smaller, not why it should be avoided.
