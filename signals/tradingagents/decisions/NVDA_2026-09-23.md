**Executive Summary**: Rate NVDA Overweight and build the position in stages instead of buying full size now. Tranche 1 is about 25–30% of target, scaled in between about 215 and 225, with nothing bought above 228. Size the whole position so that a gap down to about 199 is a loss you can accept. Time horizon is 3–6 months. If you already hold NVDA, apply the same entry, add and stop rules to any additions.

**Investment Thesis**: The business case is strong, and even the bear does not dispute it. July-quarter revenue was $96.2B, up about 106% year over year and about 18% from the prior quarter. Operating income was $63.7B, about 2.2x the year-ago level, with a 66% operating margin and 75% gross margin. Six-month operating cash flow was $74.4B. On a run-rate basis the stock trades around 23x earnings. That multiple is unconfirmed: it annualizes the peak quarter and includes non-operating income. Even with a haircut, a mid-20s multiple is not obviously stretched for a company more than doubling its operating income. The trend is also intact. Price is above the 10 EMA (222.43), the 50 SMA (215.19) and the 200 SMA (198.71), all stacked in bullish order. RSI is about 56, and the MACD histogram has turned positive again, from -1.57 to +0.49.

The Conservative and Neutral analysts won the argument on entry price and risk. Buying at 225.51 with a stop at 210.96 risks about 14.5 points to reach the May high of 235.20, about 9.7 points away. That is roughly 0.67 to 1, which undercuts the Aggressive analyst's claim of asymmetry. Filling near 218 against the same stop changes it to about 2.4 to 1. Like the Neutral analyst, I treat entry price as the main risk control, ahead of cutting size. That is why Tranche 1 is limited to the 215–225 zone and trimmed modestly to 25–30%.

The Conservative analyst also made the stronger point on liabilities. Current liabilities fell slightly, from $43.9B to $43.0B, while total liabilities rose from $64.0B to $91.3B. That means non-current liabilities went from about $20B to about $48B. Customer prepayments usually sit in current liabilities, so the Aggressive analyst's prepayment explanation is a weak fit. This is not evidence of a problem, but it is a firm condition for completing the position.

The volume requirement for Tranche 2 stays strict. The 298.9M-share breakout on August 27 reversed the next day, and the September 23 session closed in the lower third of its range on 89.5M shares. Price has also made lower highs since May: 235.20, then 230.10, then 228.87. All three are within about 3% of each other, which looks like consolidation under resistance rather than a top, but the breakout is not yet confirmed.

The rating is not Hold because nothing in the data shows a deteriorating business. The trend is up, and the open questions are verification gaps, not negative findings. The rating is not Buy because several things are missing. There is no confirmed trailing or forward P/E. The January 2026 quarter is missing, so the sequential trend rests on one data point. The cash flow figures do not fully reconcile, with a gap of about $2.6B. There is also no news, export-control, macro or insider data. For a high-multiple chip stock those gaps matter, and they create a real risk of a gap through the stop.

What would change the view. I would upgrade toward Buy on a volume-confirmed close above 230.10 and then above 235.20, along with a benign 10-Q explanation such as debt raised for buybacks, tax items or leases, and a reasonable confirmed multiple. I would downgrade to Hold or Underweight on any of these: a daily close below 210.96 or the 200 SMA; sequential revenue growth slowing sharply from about 18%; gross margin falling meaningfully below 75%; operating income growth slowing while net income is propped up by non-operating gains; or the liability build turning out to be obligations that threaten cash generation.
