**Investment Thesis**: Underweight is the right call, and the debate settled it more clearly than the conflicting rhetoric suggests. All three analysts, including the Aggressive one, support cutting to about 70% at 79,000–80,000. The real dispute is how to manage the remaining core, and on that the Conservative and Neutral analysts have the better argument.

Why reduce rather than hold:
1) Price is stalling while overextended. The 79,970 high was set on 08-24, and the next two sessions made lower highs. RSI is 80.86 after peaking at 86, and the MACD histogram has fallen two sessions running, from 1,677 to 1,421. The Aggressive analyst calls this harmless deceleration, but slowing momentum at a 25% extension is enough reason to trim. It does not need a crossover.
2) Participation is fading. Volume dropped from 74.5B to 29.3B, about 61%. The only flow evidence available, the "ETF Flows Reverse" headline, points against the rally. The Neutral analyst is right that this headline has no stated size and is a warning rather than a verdict. Still, no evidence in the debate shows inflows.
3) The VWMA argument does not support holding. The VWMA at 73,282 lags by construction and mostly reflects the 08-20 and 08-21 breakout days, not current buying.
4) The Aggressive analyst's structural case is weaker than presented. As the Neutral analyst pointed out, the 3,100-point figure is the gap still remaining between the 50 SMA and the 200 SMA, not the amount already closed. The death-cross structure has not been resolved. The 82,364 upper Bollinger Band is a volatility envelope, not a price target.
5) Volatility is high and CPI is a known binary event with no data on the result. ATR has nearly doubled to about 2,250, roughly 2.8% of price. Holding a full core through CPI without Fed, VIX or funding data means carrying risk we cannot measure.

Why this is not a Sell: price is still above the 10 EMA, the 50 SMA and the 200 SMA. The 50 SMA is rising, the MACD line is still climbing, and the breakout has held. Nothing shows that a top is in.

How the debate shaped the risk plan: the Conservative analyst showed that holding a full core down to the 10 EMA (about 1.9 ATR away) breaks the 1.5 ATR risk budget, and that a 76,527 stop is only about 1.1 ATR away and could be hit by normal noise. The Neutral analyst fixed both problems. Use daily closes rather than intraday prints, which reduces whipsaw risk at 76,527. Then size each tranche to the budget, so a smaller core can keep the 10 EMA as its last exit. Before relying on the Cryptonews "$64K before CPI" article, check its date. Until then, treat 64,000 as the level the breakout started from, not as a forecast.

Re-entry: if price pulls back to 74,800–76,500 after CPI and holds the 10 EMA on rising volume, add back to about 85%, exiting that add on a daily close below 74,800. Return to full allocation only on a daily close above 79,970 that holds into the next session, with up-day volume above about 45B and the MACD histogram turning back up.

What would change the rating: confirmed ETF inflows plus a new high on strong volume would move it to Overweight. Confirmed outflows plus a daily close below 74,800 would move it to Sell. Still missing: the size of ETF flows, on-chain valuation (MVRV, realized price, exchange flows), funding rates and open interest, the CPI date and result, VIX and the Fed path, and social sentiment. These gaps are a reason to keep positions smaller.
