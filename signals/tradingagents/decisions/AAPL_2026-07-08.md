No analyst disputes these numbers, and the bear conceded earnings aren't deteriorating. That makes the June drop look like a pullback inside an uptrend, though the conservative analyst is right that one month of indicator data can't confirm a trend lasting "months."

Why not a full Buy: The aggressive analyst claimed the $289 stop sits below the late-June lows. It doesn't; the June 25 close was 274.91 and the lower band is 275.47, so $289 is an indicator trough, not a price floor. Against the $330 reference, buying at $313 offers about 17 points of upside for 24 points of risk, worse than 1:1, while buying at $302 offers about 28 for 13, roughly 2:1. Daily volume on the advance fell from 75.4M to 53.6M, 42.5M and 41.3M shares, so the rally hasn't proven buyers are there. Valuation multiples, macro data, news, the cause of the June selloff, and insider data are all missing. I discount the bull's 230bp margin expansion and the derived ~$78B free cash flow because they depend on a suspect gross-profit mapping. The balance sheet also doesn't reconcile (equity up ~$18B despite large buybacks), so I give both issues little weight until checked.

Why not Hold or staying out: A stop at $289 exits well before a retest of 274.91, so the realistic loss on a stop-out is about 7.7%, not 12%; gap risk is real but not the base case. The breakout trigger covers the risk of missing a rally for only a few percent more than the pullback price. The data gaps argue for how to enter, not whether to.

Plan changes from the neutral analyst: a smaller starter (~1/6 of standard instead of 1/3), closing-basis stops, and one combined stop once all tranches are filled, because a third tranche at $295 with a $289 stop sits less than one ATR (8.34) from the stop. Risk controls: exit fully on a daily close below ~$289, replaced by the combined stop once filled; cut the position in half on a close below the 50 SMA together with a bearish MACD crossover.

Upgrade to Buy if the data checks come back clean, the 50 SMA holds on a retest, and price confirms a breakout above $315 on rising volume. The $330 target is only a technical reference point with no valuation work behind it, because that data isn't available.
