**Investment Thesis**: The conservative side wins, but only moderately.

The case for holding is the business. Revenue has grown about 16% year over year for three quarters, gross margin rose from 48.2% to 50.1%, free cash flow is about $110B so far this fiscal year, and the 200 SMA is rising. The bull gave no good timing reason to keep full exposure.

First, the bull's own numbers favor cutting. About 7.5% downside to the 200 SMA against 2–5% upside to the 309–311 cluster means the likely loss is bigger than the likely gain.

Second, the bull's plan contradicts itself. It says to add near 295 and also to cut on a close below 295, which the conservative and neutral analysts both pointed out.

Third, the 300 'higher low' is not confirmed as support. The July 31 gap from 333 to 304 came on about 2.5x normal volume and has not been recovered. Price is below the 10 EMA (311.20), the 50 SMA (309.23) and the VWMA (316.95). MACD is negative (-1.68, histogram about -3.3) and RSI is about 40. Quarter over quarter, revenue was flat to down (-1.6%) and EPS barely moved ($2.01 to $2.02), so the trend isn't accelerating.

We are missing valuation, the news behind the gap, macro data and the earnings date. More unseen event risk calls for a smaller position. But nothing shows the business is getting worse, so this is a trim, not a Sell.

1. The 317 'stop' above a 309 sale price doesn't protect a long. Treat it as a buyback level.
2. An 8-point stop inside an ATR of 8.53 will trigger on noise. Use daily-close triggers and about 2x ATR (~17 points) when sizing any re-entry.
