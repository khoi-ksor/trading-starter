Missing macro data is a reason to size carefully, not evidence of a macro threat.

Size: the conservative and neutral analysts win here. Gap risk is real and has already shown up in this stock. On July 31 it fell 7.3% on 132M shares, a move that could have been distribution as easily as a shakeout. A 318 stop only protects against orderly declines. A 9% gap from 330 lands near 300, below the lower Bollinger Band at 304. So position size, not the stop, is the real risk control, and the trader's half-size starter had no loss budget attached. Three other things limit conviction. Momentum is weaker than in July: MACD is 4.66 against a 9.60 peak, and the histogram fell from 2.02 to 0.42 before bouncing to 1.53. Price has stalled for four sessions just under the upper band (336.78) and the July high (339.79). And the roughly 38x trailing P/E rests on an EPS figure with one unverified quarter. A one-third starter limits a 9% gap to about 3% of the allocation's notional. That keeps exposure to the trend without the aggressive analyst's half size or the conservative analyst's wait-and-see stance. A protective put about 5–8% out of the money, 1–2 months out, is an acceptable alternative if you want to hold more than a third.

Missing data: we have no current valuation figure, no macro readings (the FRED data source wasn't available, so no rates or CPI), no news, no usable sentiment, no next earnings date and only a low-confidence cash-flow figure. The neutral analyst's point decides how to act on the earnings date. If earnings fall inside the holding window, a gap becomes a scheduled event rather than a tail risk. In that case, cut the starter further or wait until after the report. Valuation and rates can be checked manually from public sources rather than waiting for FRED.

What would change the call: upgrade to Buy, at full size, on a close above 339.79 with an expanding MACD histogram, a reasonable verified P/E and no earnings event just ahead. Cut to Hold or Underweight on a daily close below the 50 SMA (318–319), on a MACD histogram turning negative while price loses the 10 EMA, or if the valuation check comes back clearly stretched or rates turn out to be rising.
